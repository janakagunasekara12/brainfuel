Social Media

The Biggest Social Media Changes of 2025

TikTok bans, Threads growing fast, AI bots filling your feed — social media has never changed this quickly. Here's what's happening and what it means for you.

⏱ 6 min read 📅 June 2025 👤 BrainFuel Team

The TikTok Situation

TikTok has been at the centre of a major political storm. The US government passed a law in 2024 requiring TikTok's Chinese parent company, ByteDance, to sell the app or face a ban. TikTok briefly went offline for US users in January 2025, causing panic — and a surge in downloads of rival apps like RedNote and Xiaohongshu.

The situation remains unresolved in mid-2025. TikTok continues to operate in the US but faces ongoing legal challenges. Other countries — including the UK, Australia, and several EU nations — have banned TikTok from government devices over data security concerns.

📌
What this means for you:

If you are a TikTok creator, it is worth building a presence on at least one other platform — Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, or Threads — so you don't lose your audience if TikTok faces disruption.

X (formerly Twitter): A Platform Transformed

Since Elon Musk's takeover in 2022, X (formerly Twitter) has changed dramatically. Many advertisers left, the verification system was replaced with a paid subscription (X Premium), and a large portion of the original staff was let go.

By 2025, X has repositioned itself as a platform for news, political debate, and direct creator monetisation. Long-form posts, video, and paid subscriptions for creators are now central to the experience. However, many users and brands have migrated to alternatives — particularly Threads and Bluesky.

Threads: Meta's Surprise Hit

Meta launched Threads in July 2023 as a text-based alternative to X. It grew explosively — reaching 130 million monthly users within a year — and has continued to grow in 2025. Threads is connected to Instagram, so your Instagram followers can automatically follow you on Threads too.

Threads has become popular with brands, journalists, and creators who want the conversational feel of early Twitter without the controversy surrounding X. In 2025, Meta added features like a dedicated Following feed, post editing, and enhanced search.

Instagram and Facebook: Going All-In on AI

Meta has integrated AI deeply into both Instagram and Facebook. In 2025, you will encounter:

  • Meta AI assistant: Available in the search bar of both apps. You can ask it questions, get recommendations, and use it to help write captions or messages.
  • AI-generated image tools: Create backgrounds, add objects to photos, or generate entirely new images directly inside the app.
  • AI chatbots: Meta has created dozens of AI "personas" — characters based on celebrities and fictional characters that you can chat with.
  • AI content labels: Images and videos created by AI are now supposed to be labelled — though enforcement remains imperfect.

WhatsApp: More Than Just Messaging

WhatsApp now has over 2 billion users worldwide and has evolved well beyond simple chat. In 2025, key updates include:

  • Meta AI in WhatsApp: Ask AI questions directly in your chats or use it to help draft messages.
  • WhatsApp Channels: A broadcast feature allowing public figures, businesses, and organisations to send updates to followers (like a newsletter in your WhatsApp).
  • Improved privacy controls: New options to hide your IP address in calls and lock specific chats behind a password.
  • Video messages: Short video bubbles you can send in any chat — similar to voice messages but on video.

LinkedIn: The Professional Network Gets Smarter

LinkedIn — often overlooked in social media conversations — has become increasingly important in 2025. With over 1 billion members, it has added AI writing assistance for posts and job applications, AI-powered job matching, and video content feeds similar to TikTok's format. For anyone looking for a job, growing a professional brand, or learning new skills, LinkedIn is more valuable than ever.

The Rise of AI-Generated Content

One of the biggest shifts across all platforms is the explosion of AI-generated content. AI tools can now produce convincing articles, images, videos, and even synthetic voices. This creates a serious challenge:

  • It is becoming harder to know whether a photo, video, or piece of writing was created by a human or AI.
  • Misinformation spreads faster when fake content is indistinguishable from real content.
  • Platforms are introducing AI detection and labelling tools — but the technology is still imperfect.
💡
How to spot AI-generated content:

Look for unnatural-looking hands or backgrounds in images, overly perfect text with no personality, and check whether the source is credible. Tools like Google's "About this image" feature can help verify whether a photo is real.

What This All Means for You

Social media in 2025 is more powerful, more noisy, and more complicated than ever. Here's the sensible approach:

  1. Don't put all your eggs in one basket. If you create content, spread across two or three platforms.
  2. Be sceptical of what you see. Verify important information before sharing it.
  3. Use the tools that work for you. You don't need to be on every platform — pick one or two and use them intentionally.
  4. Protect your privacy. Review your privacy settings on every app you use — especially around data sharing and location.
