10 Cybersecurity Tips Everyone Should Know

Hackers are getting smarter every year. These ten simple habits will protect your accounts, devices, and personal data — no technical skills needed.

⏱ 7 min read 📅 June 2025 👤 BrainFuel Team

Why Cybersecurity Matters for Everyone

You don't need to be a celebrity or a billionaire to be targeted by cybercriminals. Hackers use automated tools to attack millions of people at once — looking for easy targets like weak passwords, unpatched software, and people who click on phishing links.

The good news is that most attacks are preventable with basic habits. You don't need to be a tech expert. You just need to know what to do.

💡
The 80/20 rule of cybersecurity:

80% of attacks can be prevented by just 20% of the protective measures. The tips in this article are that 20% — they give you the most protection for the least effort.

1. Use Strong, Unique Passwords

A strong password is long (at least 12 characters), uses a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols, and is not a real word or phrase that can be guessed. More importantly, every account should have a different password.

Why? Because data breaches happen constantly. If your email password is the same as your bank password and your email is hacked — your bank account is now also at risk.

2. Use a Password Manager

You cannot be expected to memorise dozens of unique, complex passwords. A password manager stores all your passwords securely and fills them in automatically. You only need to remember one master password.

Trusted free options include Bitwarden and KeePass. If you use Apple devices, iCloud Keychain works well. Google Password Manager is also solid for Android and Chrome users.

3. Turn On Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Two-factor authentication (also called 2-step verification) adds an extra layer of security. Even if someone steals your password, they still can't get into your account without a second piece of verification — usually a code sent to your phone or generated by an app.

Turn on 2FA for every important account: email, banking, social media, and shopping sites. Most platforms offer it in Settings > Security.

📌
Which 2FA method is safest?

An authenticator app (like Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator) is safer than receiving codes via SMS text message — because SIM-swapping attacks can intercept texts. Use an app where possible.

4. Keep Your Software Updated

Software updates are not just about new features — they almost always include security patches. When a vulnerability is discovered in your operating system, browser, or app, the developer releases a fix. If you don't install that update, you remain vulnerable to attacks that specifically target that weakness.

Enable automatic updates on your phone, computer, and apps wherever possible. Yes, updates can be annoying — but they are far less annoying than being hacked.

5. Be Very Careful with Phishing Emails

Phishing is when a criminal sends you a fake email pretending to be from a trusted source — your bank, Amazon, PayPal, or even a friend. The goal is to get you to click a link and enter your login details on a fake website.

Warning signs to look for:

  • Urgency: "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours!"
  • A sender email address that doesn't match the company (e.g. support@amazon-security-alert.com)
  • Links that look slightly wrong (hover over the link before clicking — see where it actually goes)
  • Poor grammar or spelling
  • Requests for passwords, credit card numbers, or personal information

6. Never Use Public Wi-Fi for Sensitive Activities

Public Wi-Fi in cafes, airports, and hotels is convenient — but it can be insecure. Attackers on the same network can potentially see your internet traffic. Never do online banking, log into important accounts, or enter passwords while on public Wi-Fi.

If you need to use public Wi-Fi, use a VPN (Virtual Private Network) to encrypt your connection.

7. Use a VPN When Needed

A VPN encrypts your internet traffic, making it much harder for anyone — hackers, your internet provider, or governments — to see what you are doing online. A VPN is especially useful on public Wi-Fi and when travelling.

Reputable free options include ProtonVPN (unlimited free tier) and Windscribe. Paid options like NordVPN or ExpressVPN offer faster speeds and more features.

8. Lock Your Devices

Always use a PIN, password, fingerprint, or face ID to lock your phone, tablet, and computer. If you lose your phone or it is stolen, a lock screen is the first barrier between the thief and all your accounts and data.

Also enable the "Find My Device" feature on your phone and computer so you can remotely locate or wipe the device if it is lost or stolen.

9. Back Up Your Data Regularly

Ransomware is a type of attack where criminals encrypt all your files and demand payment to unlock them. The best defence? Regular backups — so even if you are attacked, you can restore your files without paying.

Follow the 3-2-1 rule: keep 3 copies of important data, on 2 different types of storage, with 1 copy off-site (e.g. in cloud storage like Google Drive, iCloud, or Dropbox).

10. Be Cautious of What You Share Online

Criminals build profiles of potential victims using publicly available information — your name, birthday, employer, city, pet names, and family members' names. This information can be used to guess security questions, craft targeted phishing emails, or answer identity verification questions.

Review your privacy settings on social media. Think twice before posting your exact location in real time. And be cautious about sharing personal details in public forums or with apps that don't need that information.

💡
Start today:

You don't need to do all of this at once. Start with Tips 1, 2, and 3 — strong passwords, a password manager, and two-factor authentication. Those three alone will protect you against the vast majority of attacks.

Quick Wins

  • ✅ Use unique passwords
  • ✅ Get a password manager
  • ✅ Enable 2FA everywhere
  • ✅ Keep software updated
  • ✅ Think before you click
