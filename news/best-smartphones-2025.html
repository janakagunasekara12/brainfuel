Best Smartphones to Buy in 2025

iPhone, Samsung, Google Pixel — which phone is actually worth your money? We break down the top picks at every price point, with no technical jargon.

⏱ 7 min read 📅 June 2025 👤 BrainFuel Team

What to Look for in a Smartphone

Before we dive into specific phones, it helps to know what actually matters. Here are the key things to consider when buying a new smartphone:

What to checkWhy it matters
Battery lifeHow long it lasts on a single charge. Look for all-day battery life at minimum.
Camera qualityThe number of megapixels is less important than the actual photo quality in real-world tests.
Processor (chip)Determines how fast and smooth the phone runs — both now and in future years.
StorageHow much space for photos, apps, and files. 128GB is the minimum for most people.
Software updatesHow many years will the manufacturer support the phone with security and feature updates?
PriceFlagship phones cost a lot — but mid-range phones have become very capable.

Best Overall: Apple iPhone 16

For most people, the iPhone 16 is the safest and most well-rounded choice. Apple's A18 chip makes it incredibly fast, the camera system is excellent, and iOS is polished and easy to use. Apple promises at least five more years of software updates — meaning this phone will remain secure and functional well into the 2030s.

The standard iPhone 16 offers a great balance of features and price. If you want a larger screen, the iPhone 16 Plus is the same phone with a bigger display. For the best camera Apple has ever made, step up to the iPhone 16 Pro.

💡
Who should buy an iPhone?

Anyone who wants a phone that "just works", has an existing Apple ecosystem (MacBook, iPad, Apple Watch), or who values long-term software support and privacy.

Best Android: Samsung Galaxy S25

Samsung's Galaxy S25 is the best Android phone you can buy right now. It runs on Qualcomm's Snapdragon 8 Elite chip — one of the fastest mobile processors on the planet — and Samsung promises seven years of OS and security updates. The display is stunning, the cameras are versatile, and the build quality is premium.

Samsung has also packed the S25 with Galaxy AI features — tools that can summarise notes, translate calls in real time, and help you edit photos with a single tap.

Want a larger, more powerful version? The Galaxy S25 Ultra includes a built-in S Pen stylus and the best zoom camera in any smartphone.

Best for Photos: Google Pixel 9

Google's Pixel 9 is the smartphone camera champion. Google's computational photography — the software that processes images — is arguably the best in the industry. Night photography, portrait shots, and low-light videos all look exceptional. The Pixel 9 also gets seven years of Android updates directly from Google.

Google has added a suite of AI photography features — including Magic Eraser (removes objects from photos), Best Take (swaps faces from multiple shots to get everyone smiling), and Video Boost (processes video in the cloud for professional-level results).

📌
Pixel 9 vs iPhone 16 cameras:

Both are excellent. Pixel 9 tends to produce more natural, true-to-life colours. iPhone 16 produces slightly punchier, more vibrant images. It really comes down to personal preference.

Best Budget Pick: Nothing Phone (3a)

If you want a great smartphone without spending a fortune, the Nothing Phone (3a) is one of the best value phones available. It features a unique transparent back design, a clean and fast version of Android, and a solid camera system — all at roughly a third of the price of flagship phones. Battery life is particularly impressive, often lasting two full days.

Best Budget Alternative: Motorola Edge 50

Motorola has quietly become one of the best budget phone makers in the market. The Motorola Edge 50 offers a large, bright display, a long-lasting battery, and clean Android software with minimal bloatware. It won't compete with flagships on camera quality, but for calls, browsing, social media, and streaming, it does everything you need at a very reasonable price.

Which Phone Should You Buy?

  • Best overall choice: iPhone 16 — reliable, fast, long support life
  • Best Android flagship: Samsung Galaxy S25 — powerful, feature-rich
  • Best camera: Google Pixel 9 — unmatched computational photography
  • Best value: Nothing Phone (3a) or Motorola Edge 50

The most important thing is to pick a phone that fits your budget and your habits. An expensive phone is only worth it if you actually use those extra features. For most everyday users, a mid-range phone will do everything they need — brilliantly.

Quick Picks

  • 🏆 Overall: iPhone 16
  • 🤖 Android: Galaxy S25
  • 📷 Camera: Pixel 9
  • 💰 Budget: Nothing Phone (3a)
