Sorting and Filtering Data in Excel

Two of Excel's most useful everyday tools — sort your data into the right order and filter it to show only what you need, in seconds.

⏱ 9 min read 📅 Updated 2025 👤 BrainFuel Team

Why Sorting and Filtering Matter

When your spreadsheet has dozens or hundreds of rows, finding the data you need can feel like searching for a needle in a haystack. Sorting and filtering are Excel's answer to that problem.

  • Sorting rearranges your data into a meaningful order — alphabetically, by number, by date, or by colour.
  • Filtering hides rows you don't need so you can focus only on the data that matters right now.

Neither tool changes or deletes your data — it simply changes how it is displayed. You can always undo a sort or clear a filter to get back to normal.

💡
Did you know?

Sorting and filtering are among the most-used Excel features in real workplaces. If you work with lists of customers, products, employees, or orders — you will use these every day.

Sorting Data

Quick Sort (A to Z or Z to A)

The fastest way to sort a single column:

Click any cell in the column you want to sort

For example, click any cell in a "Name" column to sort alphabetically, or any cell in a "Sales" column to sort by amount.

Go to the Data tab

Click Data in the ribbon at the top of the screen.

Click A→Z or Z→A

A→Z sorts text alphabetically (A to Z) and numbers from smallest to largest. Z→A does the opposite — largest to smallest, Z to A.

📌
Important:

When you sort, Excel moves the entire row — not just the column you clicked. So if row 3 has "Alice | Sales | £4,200", the whole row stays together when sorted. Your data will not get scrambled.

Sorting by Multiple Columns

Sometimes you need to sort by more than one column — for example, sort by Department first, then by Name within each department.

Click anywhere in your data
Go to Data → Sort

This opens the Sort dialogue box.

Set your first sort level

Under "Sort by", choose your first column (e.g. Department) and the order (A to Z).

Click "Add Level" and set the second sort

Choose your second column (e.g. Name) and the order. Click OK.

Sorting by Date

Sorting dates works exactly the same way — click any cell in your date column, go to Data, and click A→Z (oldest to newest) or Z→A (newest to oldest). Excel recognises date values automatically.

Filtering Data

Turning on AutoFilter

AutoFilter adds dropdown arrows to your column headers, letting you filter your data instantly.

Click anywhere in your data
Go to Data → Filter

Small dropdown arrows appear on each column header. You can also press Ctrl + Shift + L as a shortcut.

Click a dropdown arrow to filter

Click the arrow on any column header. A menu appears showing all the unique values in that column — tick or untick them to show or hide those rows.

Filtering by a Specific Value

For example, to show only rows where Region = "North":

  1. Click the dropdown arrow on the "Region" column header.
  2. Untick "Select All" to deselect everything.
  3. Tick "North" only.
  4. Click OK. Excel hides all rows except those with "North" in the Region column.

Notice that the row numbers on the left turn blue — this tells you a filter is active. Rows that are hidden still exist; they are just not shown.

Filtering Numbers (Greater Than, Less Than)

For number columns, Excel offers smarter filter options:

  1. Click the dropdown arrow on a number column (e.g. "Sales").
  2. Hover over Number Filters.
  3. Choose an option — Greater Than, Less Than, Between, Top 10, Above Average, etc.
  4. Enter your value and click OK.

Filtering by Date

Date columns get their own smart options under Date Filters — This Week, Last Month, This Quarter, Between specific dates, and more. Excel groups dates by year and month automatically in the filter dropdown.

Using the Search Box in a Filter

If your column has many unique values, type in the search box at the top of the filter dropdown to find what you need quickly. For example, type "Smith" to find all rows where the name contains "Smith".

💡
Filter multiple columns at once

You can apply filters to more than one column at the same time. For example, filter Region = "North" AND Product = "Widget A" — Excel shows only rows that match both conditions.

Clearing a Filter

To remove a filter from one column, click its dropdown arrow and choose Clear Filter From "[Column Name]".

To remove all filters at once and show all your data again, go to Data → Clear. Your data is completely unchanged — all hidden rows come back.

To turn off AutoFilter completely (remove the dropdown arrows), press Ctrl + Shift + L again or go to Data → Filter.

Sorting & Filtering a Table

If your data is formatted as an Excel Table (Ctrl + T), the sort and filter dropdowns are added automatically. Tables also keep the filter active even when you add new rows — which is why using a Table is highly recommended for any list you work with regularly.

📌
Quick recap

Sort — Data tab → A→Z or Z→A. Rearranges rows permanently (but you can undo with Ctrl+Z).
Filter — Data tab → Filter (or Ctrl+Shift+L). Hides rows temporarily. Your data is always safe.
