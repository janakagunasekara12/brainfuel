Excel Conditional Formatting

Make your spreadsheet do the visual work for you — automatically highlight cells, spot high and low values, and make patterns jump out of your data.

⏱ 10 min read 📅 Updated 2025 👤 BrainFuel Team

What is Conditional Formatting?

Conditional Formatting automatically changes the appearance of a cell — its colour, font, or border — based on the value inside it. You set a rule, and Excel applies the formatting for you, instantly, even as your data changes.

For example, you could tell Excel: "Highlight any sales figure below £1,000 in red" or "Colour the top 10% of scores in green." Excel does this automatically for every cell in your range.

💡
Why use it?

Conditional Formatting turns a plain table of numbers into a visual dashboard — you can spot problems, outliers, and trends at a glance without reading every cell. It is one of the fastest ways to make a spreadsheet look professional and easy to understand.

Highlight Cells Rules

The most common use — highlight cells that meet a condition you choose.

Select the cells you want to format

Click and drag to select the range (e.g. B2:B20 for a column of sales figures).

Go to Home → Conditional Formatting → Highlight Cells Rules

You will see a submenu with options like Greater Than, Less Than, Between, Equal To, Text that Contains, and Duplicate Values.

Choose your condition and set the value

For example, click Greater Than, type 5000, and choose a colour (e.g. Green Fill). Click OK.

Excel immediately highlights every cell in your selection that is greater than 5000 in green. If you change a value later, the formatting updates automatically.

Useful Highlight Rules

RuleWhat it doesGood for
Greater ThanHighlights cells above a numberHigh sales, above target
Less ThanHighlights cells below a numberLow stock, below target
BetweenHighlights cells in a rangeAcceptable range values
Equal ToHighlights exact matchesSpecific status values
Text that ContainsHighlights cells with specific textFinding keywords in a list
Duplicate ValuesHighlights repeated entriesSpotting duplicates in a list

Top/Bottom Rules

Quickly highlight the best or worst performers in your data:

  • Top 10 Items — highlights the 10 highest values (you can change it to any number)
  • Bottom 10 Items — highlights the 10 lowest values
  • Top 10% — highlights the top percentage of values
  • Above Average — highlights cells above the average of the selected range
  • Below Average — highlights cells below the average

To use these: Home → Conditional Formatting → Top/Bottom Rules → choose your option.

📌
Tip:

"Top 10 Items" does not mean the top 10 — it means the top N items, and you can change N to any number in the dialog box. So "Top 3 Items" would highlight only the three highest values.

Data Bars

Data Bars add a coloured bar inside each cell, like a mini bar chart — longer bars for bigger values, shorter bars for smaller values. It gives you an instant visual comparison across a column of numbers.

To apply: Home → Conditional Formatting → Data Bars → pick a colour. Done.

This is particularly effective for comparing things like monthly revenue, product quantities, or test scores across many rows.

Colour Scales

Colour Scales apply a gradient of colours across your cells — for example, red for low values fading through yellow to green for high values. It is like a heat map.

To apply: Home → Conditional Formatting → Colour Scales → pick a scale. The most popular is the Red–Yellow–Green scale, which makes it immediately obvious which values are good and which need attention.

Icon Sets

Icon Sets add small visual symbols to cells — arrows, traffic lights, stars, or flags — based on the value. For example, a green up arrow for high values, yellow dash for middle values, and red down arrow for low values.

To apply: Home → Conditional Formatting → Icon Sets → choose a set.

💡
Which one should you use?

Use Highlight Cells Rules when you need to find specific values. Use Colour Scales or Data Bars when you want to visually compare an entire column of numbers at once. Use Icon Sets for dashboards and reports where traffic-light style indicators communicate status instantly.

Managing and Removing Rules

If you have applied multiple rules or want to edit or delete them:

Go to Home → Conditional Formatting → Manage Rules

This opens a window showing all the rules applied to your selection or the entire sheet.

Select a rule and click Edit Rule or Delete Rule

You can change the condition, the formatting colour, or the range of cells the rule applies to.

Clearing All Rules

To remove all Conditional Formatting from selected cells: Home → Conditional Formatting → Clear Rules → Clear Rules from Selected Cells.

To remove all rules from the entire sheet: choose Clear Rules from Entire Sheet.

📌
Remember:

Conditional Formatting only changes how a cell looks — it never changes the value inside. Removing it simply returns the cell to its original appearance. Nothing is lost.
