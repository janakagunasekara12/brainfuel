Creating Charts in Excel

Turn your raw data into clear, professional charts and graphs. Learn bar charts, line charts, and pie charts — step by step.

⏱ 10 min read📅 Updated 2024👤 BrainFuel Team

Why Use Charts?

A chart (also called a graph) turns numbers into a visual picture. The human brain understands images far faster than rows of numbers. With a chart, you can:

  • Spot trends at a glance (is sales going up or down?)
  • Compare values easily (which product sells most?)
  • Show proportions clearly (what share does each region have?)
  • Present data professionally in reports and presentations

Choosing the Right Chart Type

Excel offers many chart types. Here are the ones you will use most often:

Chart TypeBest Used ForExample
Column / Bar ChartComparing values across categoriesSales by product
Line ChartShowing trends over timeMonthly revenue
Pie ChartShowing parts of a whole (percentages)Market share
Area ChartTrends with volume emphasisWebsite traffic over time
Scatter ChartShowing relationships between two variablesHeight vs. weight
💡
Tip: Use Recommended Charts

Not sure which chart to use? Select your data, go to the Insert tab, and click Recommended Charts. Excel will suggest the best options based on your data.

Creating Your First Chart

Let's say you have a simple table of monthly sales. Here is how to turn it into a column chart:

Prepare your data

Make sure your data is clean and organised. Put your labels in the first column (or row) and your values next to them. For example:
Column A: Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun
Column B: 4500, 6200, 5800, 7100, 8400, 9200

Select your data

Click on the first cell of your data (e.g. A1), hold Shift, and click the last cell (e.g. B7). Both columns should now be highlighted in blue.

Go to the Insert tab

Click the Insert tab at the top of the Excel window.

Choose your chart type

In the Charts section of the ribbon, click the chart type you want. For a column chart, click the column chart icon (it looks like vertical bars). A dropdown will appear — pick the first option (Clustered Column).

Your chart appears!

Excel will instantly draw the chart on your sheet. You can drag it to a new position and resize it by dragging the corners.

Adding a Chart Title

A chart without a title is confusing. Here is how to add or change the title:

  • Click on the chart to select it
  • Click on the text that says "Chart Title" at the top of the chart
  • Delete the placeholder text and type your own title (e.g. "Monthly Sales 2024")
  • Click outside the title box when done

Adding Axis Labels

Axis labels explain what the numbers on each axis represent (e.g. "Month" on the horizontal axis, "Sales (£)" on the vertical axis).

Click the chart

Select the chart so the Chart Tools appear in the ribbon.

Click the "+" button

A small + icon appears to the right of the chart when selected. Click it to see Chart Elements.

Tick "Axis Titles"

Check the box next to Axis Titles. Labels will appear on both axes — click each one to type your label.

Formatting Your Chart

Make your chart look polished with these formatting options:

Change the Chart Style

With the chart selected, click the paintbrush icon (Chart Styles) to the right of the chart. You can browse dozens of pre-designed colour styles and apply one with a single click.

Change Bar/Line Colours

  • Click on one of the bars (or the line) in the chart to select that data series
  • Right-click and choose Format Data Series
  • Click the paint bucket icon and choose Solid Fill
  • Pick any colour you like

Adding Data Labels

Data labels show the exact value on each bar or point, making your chart even clearer:

  • Click the + button next to the chart
  • Tick Data Labels
  • The values will now appear on top of each bar (or beside each point)
📌
Pie chart tip: Show percentages

For pie charts, right-click any slice and choose Format Data Labels. Tick Percentage and untick Value to show percentages instead of raw numbers.

Moving a Chart to its Own Sheet

If your chart is getting in the way of your data, move it to a dedicated chart sheet:

  • Right-click anywhere on the chart
  • Click Move Chart
  • Select New sheet and give it a name
  • Click OK

The chart now has its own full-page tab, separate from your data.

Summary

You can now create and customise charts in Excel! Remember:

  • Choose the right chart type for your data (column for comparisons, line for trends, pie for proportions)
  • Select your data first, then insert the chart from the Insert tab
  • Always add a clear chart title and axis labels
  • Use data labels and chart styles to make your chart easier to read
  • Move the chart to its own sheet if it clutters your spreadsheet
